AdSense for a Blog: A Readiness and Measurement Checklist

A practical guide for Indian bloggers on meeting Google AdSense eligibility, implementing measurement, and managing the path to monetization.

Many bloggers spend months creating content only to face the frustration of an AdSense rejection without a clear explanation. The pain is common: you have a live site, but you lack a structured readiness checklist to ensure your site meets the specific eligibility criteria before applying. Earning from Google AdSense is not an automatic result of having a blog; it is a process of aligning your content strategy with platform policies and technical requirements. This guide provides a framework to evaluate your readiness, set up measurement, and handle the technical setup without relying on guesswork.

Quick answer

To pursue Google AdSense, check its eligibility requirements: original, useful content; access to your site HTML; an eligible adult account holder; and compliance with current program policies. Follow Google’s account and site-review instructions. Installing an ad loader or publishing ads.txt does not itself guarantee approval, ad delivery or earnings. Review the reader experience and privacy requirements before monetizing, and use actual account reports rather than assumed earnings per visit.

  • Original content and compliance with current policies are eligibility considerations; they do not guarantee approval.
  • Technical ownership of HTML source code is mandatory for standard AdSense accounts.
  • Approval is a prerequisite for earning, not a guarantee of a specific income level.
  • Policy compliance is an ongoing maintenance task, not a one-time checklist.

Where the work gets stuck

  • Receiving vague rejection notices during the AdSense application process.
  • Lack of clarity on whether content is 'unique' enough to meet quality standards.
  • Confusion between technical setup (like ads.txt) and the actual approval process.

Core Eligibility Requirements

Before submitting your application, you must verify that your site meets the fundamental baseline established by Google. This includes a strict age requirement where the applicant must be at least 18 years old. If the site owner is under 18, a parent or guardian must manage the account.

Google asks for original, high-quality content that interests readers. Review whether each page answers a real question, explains its evidence and adds useful value. Do not infer that a word count, a page total or a particular writing tool automatically satisfies a review.

  • Ownership: You must have access to the HTML source code of the site.
  • Originality: Content must be high-quality and unique to your domain.
  • Compliance: Strict adherence to AdSense Program policies is non-negotiable.
  • Age: Applicant must be 18+ or have a legal guardian manage the account.

Technical Readiness and Implementation

Separate account eligibility, site review, installation and ad serving into distinct tasks. Follow the setup instructions presented in your own AdSense account and confirm the relevant site status there. A script tag confirms only that code was included; it does not establish account approval or successful ad serving.

For a host partner such as Blogger, consult the host-specific signup guidance linked by Google. For an independent site, confirm that you can edit the required HTML and test the implementation without obscuring navigation or the article itself. Check the current consent and privacy guidance for the audience you actually serve.

  • HTML Access: Verify you can edit the <head> section of your website.
  • Ad Placement: Determine where ads will appear without disrupting the user experience.
  • Policy Updates: Establish a routine to check for changes in Google's Terms and Conditions.

Measuring Performance and Throughput

Keep separate measures for publishing work, reader engagement and ad reporting. Track how many useful updates were reviewed, which pages readers visit and whether the account reports ad impressions. Do not assume that a page view is an ad impression or infer a fixed amount of income from visitor totals.

Cost involves the time spent on content production and the potential impact of ad load on page speed. If ads slow down the site significantly, you may see a drop in user retention, which ultimately lowers the long-term earning potential.

  • Time: Track hours spent on content creation versus time spent on ad optimization.
  • Throughput: Measure the ratio of page views to ad impressions.
  • Cost: Evaluate hosting and tool costs relative to the scale of the blog.

Automation vs. Human Oversight

Automate routine reminders, broken-link checks and content inventory updates where they help. Keep a person responsible for evidence, original examples, permissions and reader experience. Automation is a publishing aid and does not establish eligibility or policy compliance.

Review every generated draft before publication. Weak or inaccurate material can harm readers regardless of the tool used to produce it. Check current publisher guidance, verify factual claims and keep a record of issues that need correction rather than assuming that passing a technical test makes a page ready for monetization.

  • Automated: Ad matching, payment processing, and basic traffic reporting.
  • Human: Content strategy, policy auditing, and user experience (UX) design.
  • Setup Effort: Moderate; involves account creation and code integration.
  • Maintenance: Continuous; requires auditing content for current policy adherence.

From friction to a smoother workflow

  1. Uncertainty about content quality and policy compliance.

    Audit all existing pages against AdSense Program policies, removing thin content and ensuring high-quality, original writing.

    Measure: Percentage of pages meeting original content standards.

  2. Technical barriers in placing the ad code or verifying ownership.

    Ensure full access to the site's HTML source code and implement the required snippet in the head section.

    Measure: Successful verification of site ownership by the AdSense crawler.

  3. Lack of visibility into which content actually drives ad impressions.

    Integrate analytics to track page views and user behavior before and after ad deployment.

    Measure: Average page views per session and bounce rates.

An illustrative example

Illustrative example: an India-based balcony-gardening publisher keeps an inventory of original articles, photo permissions and factual references. An editor checks that each page answers a useful local question and that readers can find contact and privacy information. A technical reviewer verifies the requested setup and records the account’s actual site-review status. The team logs its preparation time and outstanding issues. This hypothetical preparation process does not establish Google approval or predict ad earnings; the publisher must follow the actual review outcome and current policies.

  3. Record unresolved policy, privacy and technical questions and resolve them before approving publication.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use AdSense if I don't own the HTML code of my site?

For standard accounts, you generally need access to the HTML source code to place the AdSense snippet. However, if you use a host partner like Blogger, you can sign up for a hosted account through their specific integration process.

Does having a lot of traffic guarantee AdSense approval?

No. Traffic volume does not override the requirement for original, high-quality content and policy compliance. A high-traffic site with non-original content can still be rejected.
