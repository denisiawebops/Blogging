How to generate leads in real estate: an inquiry-to-follow-up workflow

Learn a structured approach to getting leads in real estate, from initial capture and qualification to organized follow-ups and outcome reviews.

For many professionals, the primary challenge isn't just learning how to get leads in real estate, but managing them once they arrive. Whether you are wondering how to create leads in real estate through digital marketing or how to get leads as a realtor through networking, the true value lies in the transition from a raw inquiry to a closed deal. A fragmented process often leads to missed opportunities. By implementing a standardized inquiry-to-follow-up workflow, agents can ensure that no potential client falls through the cracks. This guide explores a practical framework for managing the lead lifecycle, utilizing tools like ClawTrust Pro to maintain organization and consistency.

In this guide

  • Capturing the Initial Inquiry
  • Qualifying the Lead
  • Recording Ownership and Next Actions
  • Executing the Follow-Up
  • Reviewing Outcomes and Refining the Process

Capturing the Initial Inquiry

The first step in understanding how to generate leads in real estate is creating accessible entry points for potential clients. An inquiry can originate from various channels, such as a website contact form, a social media message, or a referral. The goal is to capture basic contact information and the nature of the request immediately.

For example, a lead might submit a request for a property valuation or inquire about a specific listing. Capturing this data in a centralized system prevents the common issue of leads being scattered across different email threads and note apps.

  • Website landing pages with clear call-to-actions
  • Social media lead generation forms
  • Referral tracking from professional networks
  • Direct inquiries from property signage

Qualifying the Lead

Once you have an inquiry, the next phase is qualification. Not every lead is ready to buy or sell immediately. Qualifying helps you determine how to prioritize your time and which resources to allocate to specific prospects.

An illustrative qualification process involves asking key questions: Is the lead a first-time buyer? Do they have a pre-approval letter? Are they looking to sell within the next three months? This ensures that your high-touch efforts are focused on the most viable opportunities.

  • Budget and financing verification
  • Timeline for moving or selling
  • Motivation for the real estate transaction
  • Property preferences and must-have features

Recording Ownership and Next Actions

To maintain a professional operation, it is essential to record who owns the lead and what the immediate next step is. In a team environment, this prevents multiple agents from contacting the same person, which can appear uncoordinated.

Every lead entry should be coupled with a specific 'Next Action.' For instance, if a lead expresses interest in a weekend open house, the next action is to send a calendar invitation and a reminder text 24 hours prior. This transforms a passive lead into an active appointment.

  • Assigning a lead owner to ensure accountability
  • Setting a clear deadline for the next interaction
  • Documenting the lead source for marketing analysis
  • Categorizing the lead by priority level

Executing the Follow-Up

The follow-up is where most real estate leads are won or lost. A consistent cadence of communication keeps the agent top-of-mind. This might include a mix of emails, phone calls, and personalized messages based on the lead's preferences.

Example follow-up sequences might include a 'Thank You' message immediately after the first call, followed by a market update or a curated list of properties a few days later. The key is to provide value rather than just checking in.

  • Immediate response to initial inquiries
  • Scheduled check-ins based on the client's timeline
  • Value-driven content delivery (e.g., local market reports)
  • Multi-channel outreach (Email, SMS, Phone)

Reviewing Outcomes and Refining the Process

The final stage of the workflow is the outcome review. By analyzing why certain leads converted while others did not, you can refine your approach to how you generate leads in real estate.

Reviewing outcomes involves marking a lead as 'Closed,' 'Lost,' or 'Nurture.' If a lead is marked as 'Nurture,' they move back into a long-term communication loop. This data-driven approach allows agents to identify which lead sources provide the highest quality prospects.

  • Analyzing conversion rates per lead source
  • Identifying common bottlenecks in the qualification phase
  • Updating nurture lists for long-term prospects
  • Refining the follow-up script based on client feedback

Frequently asked questions

How do I get real estate leads as a new agent?

New agents can start by leveraging their sphere of influence, networking within their community, and utilizing digital tools to capture inquiries. Establishing a structured workflow for following up with these initial contacts is critical for building a pipeline.

How do you generate leads in real estate effectively?

Effective lead generation involves a combination of attracting prospects through marketing and managing them through a rigorous qualification and follow-up system. Using a CRM to track next actions ensures no lead is forgotten.

What is the best way to handle lead qualification?

The best way to qualify leads is to ask specific, targeted questions regarding their budget, timeline, and motivation. This allows you to categorize leads by priority and tailor your communication to their specific needs.
